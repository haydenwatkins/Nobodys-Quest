# Guardian challenges

October 8, 2026. Family direction: Lily needs generous help, Ben wants something worth mastering, and Brayden and the parent want engaging fights. Difficulty should create more interesting play while keeping Patchling fast, responsive, cute and approachable. This document specifies the next encounter work; **a hard-mode switch is not shipped yet**.

## Three choices, rather than three separate adventures

| World object | What it changes | Why choose it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heart Lantern, already shipped | Hearts recover after a short breather, including during battle. | Keep exploring and experimenting after mistakes. |
| Guardian Lantern, already shipped | Longer warnings throughout guardian fights; extra hearts and slower boss shots after repeated retries. | More room to recognize and practice the guardian's actions. |
| Challenge Lantern, proposed | A guardian adds an authored twist with an active counter and a generous opening when countered. Ordinary travel and progression stay the same. | Enjoy turning a dangerous move into your advantage. |

Keep these independent. A player may learn the challenge with both help lights on, then put one out voluntarily. Do not silently extinguish help, increase difficulty after good play, or weaken help to protect a challenge score. The challenge is off by default. Do not show an actionable Challenge Lantern for a guardian whose alternate encounter is unfinished. Introduce the first optional challenge only after its basic counter has been demonstrated on the road; first contact must not be a three-way difficulty quiz.

The Heart Lantern is not a lesser adventure. Everyone gets the story, forms, stars, guardian gifts, world repairs and treasure pursuits. Standard difficulty must already offer satisfying active counters; adult enjoyment cannot depend on withholding basic fun from children.

## Rules for enjoyable difficulty

1. Preserve the player's enjoyable baseline: ordinary movement speed, responsive attacks, readable controls, nearby retries and useful forms. No universal enemy HP multiplier, higher travel friction, smaller text, unreliable input, withheld checkpoints or item-loss punishment.
2. Start with a familiar action. Develop it with a variation, then combine two established ideas. One new twist per guardian introduction; later phases build on that twist. Do not turn every encounter into a new controls lesson or bullet cloud.
3. Every added danger needs an active answer and an obvious payoff. A successful counter interrupts an attack, clears useful ground, cracks an existing ward or produces a real attack opening. Dodging remains a valid fallback, but cannot be the only answer to every boss.
4. A temporary restriction must be local, short and breakable. Example: a mire patch briefly gums up a step; a dash tears it open and splashes the Queen, leaving her crown exposed. The dash must remain usable while caught. Repeated slows cannot stack into immobility. If the equipped body has no dash, an ordinary attack can break the crust or the player can walk a clear route. Add no status that requires an unintroduced/unowned form, depleted resource or menu change to escape.
5. The first appearance is safe enough to experiment with. Show the response through the world: a practice patch splits when struck, a root shakes before it snaps, a bell answers a hit. Use animation, shape and sound together. Brief NPC advice can support it; paragraphs cannot substitute for a legible mechanic.
6. Boss success opens player opportunity. A hazard ends, the boss visibly reels, and both close and ranged builds can act before the next windup. A counter can shorten the fight through skill without making the uncounterned fight an endurance chore. Do not chain restrictions, damage and phase attacks without a breather.
7. Measure warnings against actual movement paths and the slowest viable build. Snapshot targets when warnings start. Cap simultaneous dangerous patterns; preserve clear routes and the visual meaning of warning versus impact. Telegraphed boss bodies remain safe outside their attack commitments.
8. Test interaction, rather than just damage output. Every twist needs a comfortable answer, a second valid approach and a recovery after failure. Prove native touch/controller input, scarce mana, a slow body, the help lights, interruption/stagger, phase changes, knockout, save/reload and rematch cleanup. Use TV viewing distance for the final human readability check.

## Rewards and the invitation to improve

- Keep power and collection opportunities equal. No hard-only strongest gift, bonus mastery rate, higher star income, unlock prerequisite or mandatory repeated farming. Ben's item aspirations must have reachable world routes with help available.
- Give mastery visible expression: a guardian's pleased reaction, a victory flourish and a camp lantern decoration earned by performing its counter. Those decorations can also be earned by practicing the same counter in the standard fight; a harder fight offers richer combinations, not a monopoly on cute rewards.
- Record a challenge clear as a personal accomplishment at that guardian's camp. A record describes the chosen pattern and counter successes. No public ranking, gold-versus-bronze label or reduced celebration for a helped clear. Clear recognition and reward payout are separate; repeated clears never repay the guardian gift.
- Before invitations, watch for demonstrated success: a counter landed, a safe recovery used, or a pattern answered. Then let the recovered guardian offer a friendly rematch once: “You snapped my roots! Want to try my branching trick?” The nearby lamp is optional. Declining does not create a quest, attention loop or progress block.
- Let children change one thing at a time: try the new pattern with help, repeat a counter, put out Guardian help, then optionally put out Heart recovery. Offer neither an automatic promotion nor repeated advice to extinguish lamps. Preserve all choices across visits.
- Lily still gets the joyful counter reactions and complete adventure with help. Ben sees a concrete trick he can learn and a visible way to express it. Adults choose the challenge because the encounter is more interesting. We cannot guarantee a particular family member will never choose help; wanting it must remain a valid choice.

## Lantern placement and clarity

Two help lights now stand outside every current dungeon/guardian-zone/trial entrance (22 approaches, including the five coastal challenges), in addition to all existing campfires and Pebble's opening pair. Worldwake masters are outdoors: their guardian caravan fires are the preparation point, not another door or teleport. House entrances and retired Worldback migration maps are not challenge entrances. Props remain walkable and must not obstruct a door, reward, sign, NPC or existing story action. Outside lamps use connected safe ground away from authored enemy attention; the Orchard's pair stays below the locked roots so it is available before ringing the bell.

The world prompt names the lamp, the action and its effect before A/tap changes it. The dock wraps completely and holds its position during approach and switching. The Heart symbol, sun symbol and steady lit/unlit glass support low reading. Each lamp reflects the same existing saved preference across the adventure; lighting one affects later regions too. No new HUD button, compulsory configuration quest or extra confirmation screen.

Future Challenge Lanterns use a distinct crossed-branch emblem and flame shape, not color alone. Add one only at completed supported approaches/camps. Its nearby placard and guardian demonstration name the actual local twist, e.g. “Branching roots · break a branch,” rather than an abstract adjective. The world shows the extra pattern when it is lit. Choosing a challenge snapshots that encounter's pattern; changing help remains available through the accessibility fallback, but world switches never steal an attack during danger. No collectible requires all help extinguished.
