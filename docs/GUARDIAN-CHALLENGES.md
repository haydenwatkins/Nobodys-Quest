## Rules for enjoyable difficulty

1. Preserve the player's enjoyable baseline: ordinary movement speed, responsive attacks, readable controls, nearby retries and useful forms. No universal enemy HP multiplier, higher travel friction, smaller text, unreliable input, withheld checkpoints or item-loss punishment.
2. Start with a familiar action. Develop it with a variation, then combine two established ideas. One new twist per guardian introduction; later phases build on that twist. Do not turn every encounter into a new controls lesson or bullet cloud.
3. Every added danger needs an active answer and an obvious payoff. A successful counter interrupts an attack, clears useful ground, cracks an existing ward or produces a real attack opening. Dodging remains a valid fallback, but cannot be the only answer to every boss.
4. A temporary restriction must be local, short and breakable. Example: a mire patch briefly gums up a step; a travelled dash tears it open and leaves a lily-lined approach while the Queen keeps fighting. The dash must remain usable while caught. Repeated slows cannot stack into immobility. If the equipped body has no dash, an ordinary attack can break the crust or the player can walk a clear route. Add no status that requires an unintroduced/unowned form, depleted resource or menu change to escape.
5. The first appearance is safe enough to experiment with. Show the response through the world: a practice patch splits when struck, a root shakes before it snaps, a bell answers a hit. Use animation, shape and sound together. Brief NPC advice can support it; paragraphs cannot substitute for a legible mechanic.
6. Boss success opens player opportunity. A response creates useful ground, carries protection, changes remaining pressure or buys recovery; both close and ranged builds can use the result. A counter can shorten the fight through skill without making the uncounterned fight an endurance chore. Do not chain restrictions, damage and phase attacks without a breather.
7. Measure warnings against actual movement paths and the slowest viable build. Snapshot targets when warnings start. Cap simultaneous dangerous patterns; preserve clear routes and the visual meaning of warning versus impact. Telegraphed boss bodies remain safe outside their attack commitments.
8. Test interaction, rather than just damage output. Every twist needs a comfortable answer, a second valid approach and a recovery after failure. Prove native touch/controller input, scarce mana, a slow body, the help lights, interruption/stagger, phase changes, knockout, save/reload and rematch cleanup. Use TV viewing distance for the final human readability check.

## Rewards and the invitation to improve

- Keep power and collection opportunities equal. No hard-only strongest gift, bonus mastery rate, higher star income, unlock prerequisite or mandatory repeated farming. Ben's item aspirations must have reachable world routes with help available.
- Give mastery visible expression: a guardian's pleased reaction, a victory flourish and a camp lantern decoration earned by performing its counter. Those decorations can also be earned by practicing the same counter in the standard fight; a harder fight offers richer combinations, not a monopoly on cute rewards.
- Record a challenge clear as a personal accomplishment at that guardian's camp. A record describes the chosen pattern and counter successes. No public ranking, gold-versus-bronze label or reduced celebration for a helped clear. Clear recognition and reward payout are separate; repeated clears never repay the guardian gift.
- Before invitations, watch for demonstrated success: a counter landed, a safe recovery used, or a pattern answered. Then let the recovered guardian offer a friendly rematch once: “You snapped my roots! Want to try my branching trick?” The nearby lamp is optional. Declining does not create a quest, attention loop or progress block.
- Let children change one thing at a time: try the new pattern with help, repeat a counter, put out Guardian help, then optionally put out Heart recovery. Offer neither an automatic promotion nor repeated advice to extinguish lamps. Preserve all choices across visits.
- Lily still gets the joyful counter reactions and complete adventure with help. Ben sees a concrete trick he can learn and a visible way to express it. Adults choose the challenge because the encounter is more interesting. We cannot guarantee a particular family member will never choose help; wanting it must remain a valid choice.
